The mental load and the partner

57 videos analysed · 4 faceless

What mums are saying

The load is knowing, noticing and having to ask, not doing the task. It comes out as a grievance about the partner, and tools, apps or AI come up in about 2% of comments.

  • "I don't need him to do more, I need him to KNOW more" (115K likes)
  • "Having to ask is ANOTHER TASK" (39K likes)
  • "We don't want to be project managers." (32K likes)

What performs

Stakes and single named moments travel: a divorce confession (12.7M views, 339x), the toothpaste and dinner loops, a dad confessing his own load (6.5M and 7.2M). Plain explainers that define the mental load sit at 0.5x to 0.8x.

Childcare, money and going back to work

46 videos analysed · 8 faceless

What mums are saying

In Australia extra work days are judged net of daycare, and entitlement rules (CCS, PPL, Centrelink) are learned from strangers in comment threads.

  • "after tax, extra daycare and HECS, I'd only be about $50 a week" (Reddit AU)
  • "Anything child care related is always first in, best dressed." (Whirlpool)
  • "do we have to do anything to claim it or is it automatic??" (TikTok comment)

What performs

Dated "what's changing" explainers are the breakout format for AU money creators (top 10 at 24x to 193x; fromTash's PPL post hit 651K views on 8.7K followers). They get saved and shared, not liked. Childcare keywords have small TikTok reach but the highest comment ratios.

AI, apps and tech

41 videos analysed · 11 faceless

What mums are saying

Parents rarely raise AI unprompted. Where the topic is AI, interest is real but split, and it stops at the last step (send, book, add to calendar).

Demos that show a finished job get saved and shared. AI in the caption underperforms (Mother Mode's AI posts ran at about half its other posts), and the "AI for moms" content that travels mostly mocks mums who trust AI.

What the numbers say

  • Talking head is the conversation format. Highest comment rate (0.097% of views against 0.049% for all videos), with above-median saves and shares. It carries the load, money and expert topics.
  • Tutorials and demos are the keep format. Highest save rate of any format (0.96% against 0.48%).
  • Skits are the send format. Share rate 1.13%, about double any other format; 6.1x usual likes on Instagram.
  • Green screen over a page or post is the explainer format. Good on saves (0.76%), shares (0.59%) and comments; it is how AU money creators explain CCS and PPL changes.

 

  • Text over b-roll buys reach, not depth. 146x usual views but the lowest save (0.29%) and share (0.27%) rates. Loops travel and are forgotten.
  • Voiceover over footage saves well (0.81%) and on Instagram carries the biggest comment funnels (median about 100x usual comments, 7 posts, mostly keyword funnels).
  • Podcast clips and trend audio sit below the median on saves and shares.

What works for assistant content

What the assistant side shows

  • The audience sits under other people's words. Median TikTok plays: "ai agent" 485,900, "chatgpt for moms" 115,700, "house manager" 68,300, "life admin" 53,400; "family assistant" 4,064, "ai life admin" 630.
  • No family-assistant brand has earned organic reach. Ollie has 336 TikTok followers; Nori, Ohai and Fambot post to medians of 110 to 235 views, and their big numbers are paid. The only AU brand with reach is Mother Mode, through founder-face "who notices" loops that show no product.
  • Paid posts buy plays, not people: 0.43% likes and 0.09% saves on flagged ads against 3.74% and 0.64% organic.
  • Teaching the set-up beats brands demoing their own product: how-to and DIY posts saved at 3.51% against 0.44% for brand posts.

 

  • Show the approval moment. Posts that show it had about twice the saves and two to three times the shares (observational; holds across the cuts we tried in the saved data).
  • The household-as-a-company frame travels furthest, with no AI in it. "I am the CEO of my household" 2.4M views and 100.8K shares; a stay-at-home mum's binder 2.4M and 87K saves. Commenters want the binder but say they'd never keep it up: the opening for an agent.
  • Faceless works for proof, not reach: 4.5x baseline against 7.0x with a face, but slightly more saves and shares. The faceless winners are screen recordings of a real job (an audible AI call, a WhatsApp auto-reply).
  • A personified, all-powerful assistant draws fear; a brief, useful one draws "I want this". Top comment on the Alexa+ ad: the start of a horror film (25.7K likes). Australian assistant content is almost absent.
